# Body

## Purpose

Define realistic human body characteristics that support character identity, storytelling, and visual authenticity.

The body should not be treated as a perfect mannequin. Realistic characters contain:

- natural proportions
- physical imperfections
- age-related details
- individual traits
- environmental influence

Body appearance should communicate:

- personality
- background
- lifestyle
- emotional state
- physical condition

---

# Overall Body Realism

## Description

A realistic body should contain natural variation rather than idealized perfection.

## Characteristics

- natural body proportions
- subtle asymmetry
- realistic skin texture
- visible muscle structure when appropriate
- natural posture
- believable physical presence

## Best For

- realistic characters
- cinematic portraits
- dramatic storytelling
- historical settings
- documentary aesthetics

## Avoid When

- intentionally stylized characters
- fantasy creatures
- exaggerated animation styles

## Character Principle

Human bodies tell stories. Physical appearance should reflect a character's experiences, environment, age, and personality rather than existing only for visual attractiveness.

## Example

A traveler may have sun-exposed skin, slightly tired posture, and practical physical conditioning from long journeys.

---

# Posture

## Description

Posture describes how a character naturally holds and moves their body.

## Characteristics

- shoulder position
- spine alignment
- head angle
- body tension
- confidence or hesitation
- movement rhythm

## Best For

- showing personality
- revealing emotional state
- establishing character presence

## Avoid When

- body language contradicts the intended personality

## Character Principle

Posture communicates psychology before dialogue. A confident character and an anxious character can look completely different even with identical facial expressions.

## Example

A powerful leader may stand upright with open shoulders, while an uncertain character may avoid eye contact with slightly lowered posture.

---

# Hands

## Description

Hands are highly expressive body details that reveal personality, occupation, and emotional state.

## Characteristics

- finger movement
- hand tension
- skin texture
- scars
- calluses
- jewelry
- gestures

## Best For

- emotional close-ups
- character storytelling
- occupation details

## Avoid When

- unnecessary hand emphasis distracts from the scene

## Character Principle

Hands often reveal hidden information. They show what a character does, how they feel, and how they interact with the world.

## Example

A musician may have long fingers and subtle calluses, while a warrior may have scars and hardened skin.

---

# Arms and Shoulders

## Description

Arms and shoulders communicate strength, vulnerability, and physical history.

## Characteristics

- muscle definition
- shoulder width
- tension
- scars
- posture relationship

## Best For

- action characters
- athletes
- warriors
- physically active professions

## Avoid When

- physical appearance should not define the entire character

## Character Principle

The upper body reflects lifestyle and physical experience. It should match what the character has done throughout their life.

## Example

A soldier may have developed shoulders and restrained movements from years of training.

---

# Neck and Throat

## Description

The neck area provides subtle indicators of emotion, age, and physical condition.

## Characteristics

- muscle tension
- visible veins
- throat movement
- skin texture
- posture relationship

## Best For

- emotional intensity
- physical exertion
- dramatic close-ups

## Avoid When

- excessive anatomical detail distracts from the character

## Character Principle

Small physical reactions often communicate emotion more realistically than exaggerated expressions.

## Example

A nervous character may swallow visibly, while an angry character may show tension around the neck muscles.

---

# Skin Texture

## Description

Realistic skin contains natural variation and physical history.

## Characteristics

- pores
- fine lines
- uneven coloration
- freckles
- scars
- sun exposure
- natural shine

## Best For

- realistic characters
- cinematic close-ups
- historical settings

## Avoid When

- artificial beauty aesthetics are intended

## Character Principle

Perfect skin often reduces realism. Skin details make characters feel alive and connected to the physical world.

## Example

An outdoor worker may have weathered skin, while a sheltered aristocrat may have softer skin with fewer environmental marks.

---

# Scars and Marks

## Description

Physical marks can communicate past experiences and personal history.

## Characteristics

- healed scars
- birthmarks
- injuries
- tattoos
- distinctive features

## Best For

- character backstory
- visual identity
- dramatic storytelling

## Avoid When

- adding random details without narrative purpose

## Character Principle

A physical mark should suggest a story rather than exist as decoration.

## Example

A faded scar across a character's hand suggests previous hardship without requiring explanation.

---

# Body Age

## Description

The body should reflect the character's age realistically.

## Characteristics

### Young characters

- smoother skin
- energetic posture
- faster movements

### Middle-aged characters

- subtle lines
- mature posture
- accumulated physical history

### Older characters

- wrinkles
- reduced muscle tone
- slower movement
- age-related texture

## Best For

- realistic character design
- historical storytelling

## Avoid When

- intentionally creating ageless fantasy characters

## Character Principle

Age appears through many small details rather than a single feature.

## Example

An older character is defined not only by wrinkles but by posture, movement speed, and physical presence.

---

# Body Language

## Description

Body language describes unconscious physical communication.

## Characteristics

- gestures
- distance from others
- movement confidence
- tension
- relaxation

## Best For

- emotional storytelling
- relationships
- psychological characterization

## Avoid When

- gestures become exaggerated or theatrical without reason

## Character Principle

The body often reveals emotions that the face hides.

## Example

A character may smile politely while their hands remain tense, revealing discomfort.

---

# Physical Condition

## Description

A character's body should reflect their lifestyle and current condition.

## Characteristics

- fatigue
- injuries
- strength
- weakness
- exhaustion
- energy level

## Best For

- action scenes
- survival stories
- realistic drama

## Avoid When

- physical state contradicts the story

## Character Principle

The body is a record of experience. Physical condition should change naturally based on what the character has endured.

## Example

A character who has spent days without sleep should show fatigue through posture, eyes, movement, and skin condition.

---

# Body Selection Rules

If realism is the priority:

Prefer:

- natural proportions
- visible texture
- subtle imperfections
- believable posture

If character identity is important:

Emphasize:

- hands
- posture
- scars
- physical habits

If emotion is important:

Emphasize:

- body tension
- breathing
- small physical reactions

Avoid:

- flawless mannequin-like bodies
- random anatomical details
- unrealistic physical traits without narrative purpose
