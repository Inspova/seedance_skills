# Optical Effects

## Purpose

This skill governs optical effects for Seedance.

Optical effects simulate the physical behavior and imperfections of real-world camera lenses and transparent materials. Unlike image finishing, which refines the overall presentation, optical effects recreate the characteristics of photographic optics to increase realism, cinematic style, or artistic expression.

Its purpose is to apply physically inspired lens phenomena that enhance visual depth, realism, and cinematic atmosphere while remaining consistent with the scene's established visual language.

# When to Use

Apply this skill when the user explicitly requests optical lens behavior or when realistic optical phenomena naturally support the creative goal.

Typical indicators include:

- anamorphic lens flares
- cinematic lens flares
- chromatic aberration
- color fringing
- reflections
- mirror reflections
- wet surfaces
- glass reflections
- refractions
- transparent materials
- crystal
- water distortion
- optical realism

Do not activate this skill simply because the user requests a cinematic image.

Only apply optical effects when they naturally arise from the scene or intentionally reinforce the desired visual style.

---

# Application Philosophy

Treat optical effects as natural consequences of photography rather than decorative visual overlays.

Their influence should reinforce:

- physical realism
- lens authenticity
- environmental interaction
- cinematic immersion
- visual depth
- optical consistency

Every optical effect should appear to originate from believable physical conditions.

Favor subtle realism over exaggerated spectacle.

Avoid combining multiple strong optical effects unless intentionally pursuing a stylized visual identity.

---

# Visual Translation

Rather than outputting only:

```
Anamorphic Lens Flare
```

or

```
Chromatic Aberration
```

translate the selected effect into visible photographic behavior.

Describe elements such as:

- horizontal light streaks
- subtle optical color fringing
- realistic environmental reflections
- physically accurate refraction
- believable lens interaction
- natural light behavior
- immersive optical realism

The effect should be described through photographic characteristics rather than technical terminology alone.

---

# Style Boundaries

Avoid treating optical effects as visual decorations.

Every effect should remain consistent with:

- camera system
- lens choice
- lighting
- environment
- cinematic style
- image finishing

Avoid exaggerated lens artifacts that distract from storytelling unless intentionally requested.

Physical plausibility should remain the default.

---

# Default Behavior

If no optical effects are requested and none naturally arise from the scene:

- preserve clean optics
- avoid artificial lens artifacts
- maintain realistic image quality
- simulate professional photographic equipment

The image should feel naturally photographed rather than digitally processed.

---

# Final Principle

Optical effects should emerge naturally from the interaction between light, lenses, and materials.

Their purpose is to strengthen realism, immersion, and cinematic authenticity—not to call attention to themselves.

The best optical effects feel like they were captured in-camera rather than added afterward.
