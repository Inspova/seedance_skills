# Film Noir

## Purpose

Film Noir is a cinematic movement built around secrecy, uncertainty, psychological tension, and moral ambiguity.

It is not simply a black-and-white style, dark atmosphere, or collection of crime-related objects. Film Noir transforms the entire cinematic world into a place where information is hidden, motives are unclear, and characters exist between truth and deception.

For Seedance, Film Noir should create the feeling of watching a forgotten frame from a mid-century crime film: a world shaped by shadow, isolation, danger, and unresolved questions.

---

# Cinematic Law

Film Noir transforms reality into a world where truth is partially hidden.

Darkness conceals information.
Light reveals only fragments.
Composition creates suspicion.
Atmosphere creates uncertainty.

Every visual decision should suggest that something important exists outside the frame or remains unseen.

The audience should feel they are observing a world of secrets.

---

# Core Visual Priorities

When interpreting Film Noir, prioritize artistic decisions in this order:

1. Photographic identity and tonal rendering
2. Lighting and shadow structure
3. Atmosphere
4. Composition
5. Camera behavior
6. Production design
7. Character presentation
8. Editing

If compromises are necessary, preserve the earlier priorities.

A convincing Film Noir image depends more on photographic character and lighting than on props or costumes.

---

# Historical Inspiration

Film Noir emerged during the 1940s and 1950s, influenced by:

- German Expressionism
- hard-boiled detective fiction
- post-war uncertainty
- urban crime narratives
- moral pessimism

Classic noir should not be copied as a historical costume. Instead, preserve its cinematic language:

- high contrast
- psychological ambiguity
- urban isolation
- visual restraint
- fatalistic atmosphere

---

# Photographic Identity

The image should feel photographed through a historical cinematic process rather than captured by a modern digital camera.

Favor:

- silver-rich black-and-white photography
- authentic monochrome tonal separation
- deep blacks
- controlled highlight roll-off
- bright specular highlights
- soft highlight bloom
- subtle halation
- vintage lens softness
- optical imperfections
- analog film grain
- slight gate weave
- archival print texture
- gentle diffusion

Avoid:

- clean digital sharpness
- modern HDR
- sterile clarity
- contemporary commercial polish
- artificial monochrome filters

The goal is not simply black-and-white.

The goal is the photographic feeling of classic noir cinema.

---

# Visual Language

Film Noir emphasizes contrast, concealment, and emotional distance.

Common characteristics include:

- deep shadow-filled compositions
- partially hidden faces
- dramatic silhouettes
- narrow beams of light
- hard geometric shadows
- reflective surfaces
- smoke-filled interiors
- rain-soaked streets
- lonely urban spaces
- visual barriers
- layered foreground elements
- characters separated by architecture
- empty negative space
- oppressive environments

Every frame should feel like it contains information that is deliberately withheld.

---

# Lighting

Lighting defines the psychological world of Film Noir.

Favor:

- extreme low-key lighting
- hard directional sources
- chiaroscuro contrast
- practical lamps
- desk lamps
- streetlights
- window light
- venetian blind shadows
- doorframe shadows
- staircase shadows
- dramatic backlighting
- isolated pools of illumination
- smoke catching light beams

Darkness should dominate the image.

Avoid:

- flat illumination
- soft commercial lighting
- evenly exposed environments

Shadow is not decoration.

Shadow controls what the viewer is allowed to know.

---

# Camera

The camera should observe secrets rather than create spectacle.

Favor:

- static compositions
- slow push-ins
- restrained dolly movement
- deliberate tracking
- locked-off frames
- expressive close-ups
- over-the-shoulder framing
- hidden viewpoints
- low-angle power compositions
- high-angle vulnerability compositions

Avoid:

- energetic handheld movement
- flashy camera choreography
- action-film coverage
- excessive camera motion

The camera should feel patient, suspicious, and controlled.

---

# Composition

Composition should create psychological isolation.

Favor:

- heavy negative space
- characters framed through windows
- mirrors
- reflections
- foreground obstructions
- doorways
- corridors
- staircases
- silhouettes
- off-center framing
- visual entrapment
- geometric shadow patterns

Characters should often appear trapped, watched, or separated.

---

# Production Design

Typical environments include:

- detective offices
- dim hotel rooms
- smoky bars
- rain-soaked streets
- abandoned warehouses
- alleyways
- apartments
- industrial districts
- train stations
- empty diners
- nighttime city streets

Useful period details:

- rotary telephones
- vintage automobiles
- worn furniture
- ceiling fans
- typewriters
- old elevators
- cigarette smoke
- reflective glass

Modern settings are acceptable only when they preserve noir's emotional and visual language.

---

# Atmosphere

Film Noir atmosphere should feel oppressive but restrained.

Favor:

- rain
- fog
- smoke
- mist
- wet pavement
- distant traffic
- silence
- loneliness
- paranoia
- exhaustion
- suspicion
- fatalism
- quiet menace

The danger should feel psychological before physical.

---

# Editing

Editing should preserve tension through restraint.

Favor:

- classical continuity
- measured pacing
- lingering reactions
- gradual suspense
- invisible editing
- deliberate scene progression

Avoid:

- rapid cutting
- music-video rhythm
- action montage
- flashy transitions

---

# Characters

Film Noir characters exist within moral uncertainty.

Common archetypes include:

- detectives
- fugitives
- criminals
- corrupt officials
- femme fatales
- desperate lovers
- isolated witnesses
- morally conflicted protagonists

Performance should be:

- restrained
- emotionally controlled
- psychologically layered
- ambiguous

Avoid exaggerated theatrical emotion.

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid confusing Film Noir with generic dark cinema.

Film Noir is not:

- any black-and-white image
- any detective story
- any rainy street
- any shadowy scene

Avoid:

- modern digital appearance
- glossy cinematography
- bright environments
- clean commercial aesthetics
- colorful grading
- superhero-style action
- excessive CGI
- fantasy aesthetics
- visually comfortable compositions

If the image feels modern, clean, and perfectly polished, the noir identity has weakened.

---

# Prompt Construction

Do not rely only on:

'''Film Noir'''


Translate it into explicit cinematic decisions:

- silver-rich monochrome photography
- vintage optical character
- analog film texture
- deep shadow structure
- selective illumination
- atmospheric urban environments
- restrained camera movement
- psychological isolation
- moral ambiguity
- hidden information within the frame

The prompt should remain recognizable as Film Noir even if the label is removed.
