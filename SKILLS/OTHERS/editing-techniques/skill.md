# Editing

## Purpose

This module governs all editing and temporal storytelling decisions for Seedance.

Its purpose is to produce editing that is clear, intentional, and emotionally effective. Editing should control rhythm, continuity, and the audience's perception of time while supporting the narrative.

This module references:

- **editing.md** — a library of cinematic editing techniques and transitions.

---

# Intent

Think like a professional film editor.

Every cut should have a narrative purpose. Editing should shape pacing, reveal information, create emotion, and maintain visual coherence.

Prefer purposeful editing over unnecessary stylistic complexity.

---

# Editing Contract

Editing should follow these principles:

- preserve narrative clarity
- maintain spatial and temporal coherence unless intentionally disrupted
- support emotional pacing
- use transitions that reinforce the story
- avoid distracting or arbitrary edits

Unless explicitly requested, editing should feel invisible rather than attention-seeking.

---

# Decision Process

When designing the edit, follow this order.

## 1. Is a cut necessary?

If a scene benefits from uninterrupted continuity, prefer a continuous shot or long take.

Otherwise, determine where cuts improve storytelling.

---

## 2. What should the edit accomplish?

Examples:

- compress time
- increase tension
- reveal information
- connect scenes
- emphasize emotion
- compare simultaneous events

Select the appropriate technique from **editing.md**.

---

## 3. Should continuity be preserved?

If maintaining realism is important, prioritize seamless continuity.

If emotional impact or stylization is more important, allow discontinuity where appropriate.

---

## 4. How should the audience experience time?

Determine whether time should feel:

- continuous
- compressed
- expanded
- fragmented
- simultaneous

Choose editing techniques that reinforce this perception.

---

# Editing Hierarchy

When editing decisions conflict, prioritize:

1. Storytelling
2. Emotional pacing
3. Continuity
4. Rhythm
5. Stylistic expression

Lower priorities should never compromise higher priorities.

---

# Continuity

Within a sequence:

- maintain clear spatial relationships
- preserve screen direction unless intentionally broken
- ensure transitions feel motivated
- avoid confusing temporal jumps

Intentional discontinuity should always communicate a narrative purpose.

---

# Default Behaviour

If no editing style is specified:

- use invisible continuity editing
- maintain natural pacing
- use straightforward cuts
- avoid excessive transitions or effects

---

# Output

When describing editing, specify only techniques that materially influence the scene.

Where applicable, include:

- editing technique
- transition
- pacing
- temporal effect
- narrative purpose

Avoid unnecessary stylistic editing that distracts from the story.

---

# Editing Philosophy

Editing is the language of time.

Every cut changes how the audience experiences emotion, information, and rhythm.

Choose editing techniques that support the story rather than draw attention to themselves.
