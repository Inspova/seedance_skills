# Clothes

## Purpose

Define clothing, accessories, and wearable details that support character identity and visual storytelling.

Clothing should never exist only as decoration.

A character's clothing communicates:

- personality
- occupation
- social position
- cultural background
- historical period
- lifestyle
- emotional state
- character development

The best costume design tells the audience who a character is before they speak.

---

# Clothing Philosophy

## Description

Clothing should feel like something the character would realistically choose, own, maintain, and wear.

## Characteristics

- appropriate materials
- believable construction
- consistent style
- realistic wear
- meaningful details

## Best For

- character-driven storytelling
- cinematic realism
- world building

## Avoid When

- clothing exists only for visual attractiveness
- details contradict character background

## Character Principle

Costume design should reveal information about the character rather than simply making them visually appealing.

## Example

A wealthy noble character may wear carefully maintained fabrics and decorative accessories, while a traveler may wear practical layered clothing with visible wear.

---

# Silhouette

## Description

Silhouette refers to the overall shape and outline created by clothing.

## Characteristics

- overall proportions
- clothing volume
- shoulder shape
- waist structure
- length and layering
- movement behavior

## Best For

- immediate character recognition
- fantasy designs
- historical costumes
- iconic characters

## Avoid When

- silhouette overwhelms character personality

## Character Principle

The silhouette is often the first thing viewers recognize. A strong silhouette creates memorable visual identity.

## Example

A military commander may have a structured silhouette with sharp lines, while an artist may have softer flowing shapes.

---

# Fabric and Material

## Description

Fabric choice affects realism, movement, texture, and social meaning.

## Characteristics

### Natural fabrics

- cotton
- linen
- wool
- silk
- leather

### Synthetic materials

- technical fabrics
- plastics
- modern composites

## Best For

- establishing era
- showing lifestyle
- adding realism

## Avoid When

- material contradicts the world setting

## Character Principle

Materials reveal resources, environment, and social status.

## Example

A silk robe suggests wealth and refinement, while rough wool clothing suggests practicality and harsh conditions.

---

# Clothing Texture

## Description

Surface details make clothing feel physically real.

## Characteristics

- wrinkles
- stitching
- embroidery
- fabric grain
- folds
- damage
- aging

## Best For

- close-ups
- cinematic realism
- historical settings

## Avoid When

- excessive texture distracts from the character

## Character Principle

Perfectly smooth clothing often looks artificial. Real clothing carries evidence of movement and use.

## Example

A warrior's coat may show repaired seams, dust, and worn edges from repeated battles.

---

# Color and Pattern

## Description

Clothing colors and patterns influence character perception and emotional association.

## Characteristics

- dominant colors
- contrast
- patterns
- symbolic colors
- cultural meanings

## Best For

- visual storytelling
- character differentiation
- emotional atmosphere

## Avoid When

- colors conflict with the intended mood

## Character Principle

Color choices can communicate personality before dialogue.

## Example

A character wearing muted earth tones may appear grounded and practical, while vibrant colors may suggest confidence or extravagance.

---

# Historical Clothing

## Description

Historical clothing should reflect the period, culture, technology, and social structure of the world.

## Characteristics

- authentic materials
- period silhouettes
- appropriate accessories
- realistic construction methods

## Best For

- historical films
- fantasy worlds inspired by history
- period dramas

## Avoid When

- mixing unrelated eras without intention

## Character Principle

Historical clothing is not just appearance; it represents the society the character belongs to.

## Example

A medieval noble and a modern executive may both represent power, but their clothing communicates authority differently.

---

# Formalwear

## Description

Formal clothing communicates ceremony, status, professionalism, or social expectation.

## Characteristics

- structured design
- refined materials
- careful presentation
- decorative details

## Best For

- weddings
- ceremonies
- elite characters
- important events

## Avoid When

- formal clothing does not match the character's role

## Character Principle

Formalwear represents how characters want to be perceived by society.

## Example

A politician wearing a perfectly tailored suit communicates control and public image.

---

# Casualwear

## Description

Everyday clothing reveals a character's normal lifestyle.

## Characteristics

- comfort
- personal preference
- repeated use
- practical choices

## Best For

- daily scenes
- character introduction
- realistic storytelling

## Avoid When

- casual clothing feels disconnected from personality

## Character Principle

A character's everyday clothing often reveals more about them than special outfits.

## Example

A creative person may personalize ordinary clothing with unique combinations and accessories.

---

# Workwear

## Description

Work clothing reflects occupation, environment, and daily activities.

## Characteristics

- practical materials
- protective elements
- tools
- signs of repeated use

## Best For

- professions
- world building
- realistic characters

## Avoid When

- clothing does not match the job

## Character Principle

A person's work leaves physical traces on their clothing.

## Example

A mechanic's clothing may include oil stains, reinforced fabric, and practical pockets.

---

# Armor and Protective Clothing

## Description

Protective clothing communicates danger, profession, and physical readiness.

## Characteristics

- defensive structure
- functional materials
- weight and movement restrictions
- damage marks

## Best For

- warriors
- soldiers
- explorers
- science fiction characters

## Avoid When

- armor looks decorative but impractical

## Character Principle

Protective clothing should appear designed for survival, not only visual impact.

## Example

A soldier's armor should show wear, repairs, and practical attachment points.

---

- jackets
- robes
- capes

## Best For

- strong silhouettes

A long coat creates a powerful silhouette and suggests confidence or mystery.

## Characteristics

- material
- wear

- style
- movement support

## Best For

- character details

- environmental storytelling

# Accessories

## Description

Accessories are small wearable objects that add identity and narrative meaning.

## Characteristics

- jewelry
- watches
- weapons
- bags
- glasses
- hats
- hair ornaments
- symbolic objects

## Best For

- character recognition
- personal history
- cultural identity

## Avoid When

- accessories are random decoration

## Character Principle

The best accessories suggest ownership, memory, or personal significance.

## Example

A worn necklace may represent a lost relationship or important personal history.

---

# Jewelry and Decorative Items

## Description

Jewelry can communicate wealth, culture, personality, and relationships.

## Characteristics

- material quality
- craftsmanship
- symbolism
- aging
- personal attachment

## Best For

- fantasy
- historical characters
- emotional storytelling

## Avoid When

- jewelry has no connection to the character

## Character Principle

A single meaningful object is often stronger than many decorative objects.

## Example

A simple ring can reveal more about a character than an entire collection of expensive jewelry.

---

# Clothing Condition

## Description

The state of clothing reflects the character's recent experiences.

## Characteristics

### New clothing

- clean
- structured
- carefully maintained

### Used clothing

- wrinkles
- fading
- minor damage

### Damaged clothing

- tears
- dirt
- stains
- repairs

## Best For

- storytelling
- action scenes
- emotional transitions

## Avoid When

- clothing condition contradicts recent events

## Character Principle

Clothing records a character's journey.

## Example

A previously elegant outfit becoming damaged after a battle visually communicates transformation.

---

# Costume Selection Rules

If realism is the priority:

Prefer:

- believable materials
- appropriate wear
- practical construction
- consistent styling

If personality is important:

Emphasize:

- silhouette
- color choices
- accessories
- personal objects

If world building is important:

Emphasize:

- historical accuracy
- cultural details
- materials
- social signals

Avoid:

- random fashionable elements
- impossible materials
- excessive decoration without meaning

---

# Final Principle

Clothing is character storytelling.

A strong costume should answer:

- Who is this person?
- What world do they belong to?
- What have they experienced?
- How do they want others to see them?
