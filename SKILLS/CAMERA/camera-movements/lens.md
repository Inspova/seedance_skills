# Lenses

## Purpose

Choose a lens based on the emotional and visual intention of the scene.

Lens choice affects perspective, spatial compression, depth of field, subject isolation, and how viewers emotionally perceive a scene.

---

## 24mm Wide

### Description

A wide-angle lens that captures expansive environments while exaggerating perspective and foreground depth.

### Characteristics

- expansive field of view
- exaggerated perspective
- strong foreground-background separation
- immersive spatial feeling
- environmental storytelling

### Best For

- establishing shots
- architecture
- travel
- action
- immersive POV
- large interiors

### Avoid When

- facial distortion is undesirable
- intimate portraits
- close-up beauty shots

### Cinematography Principle

A 24mm lens emphasizes the relationship between subjects and their surroundings. Nearby objects appear larger while distant objects recede dramatically, making movement through space feel dynamic and immersive.

### Example

A 24mm lens emphasizes the scale of a cathedral while keeping the character surrounded by the architecture.

---

## 35mm

### Description

A moderately wide lens that balances environmental context with natural-looking subjects.

### Characteristics

- natural perspective
- environmental context
- subtle depth exaggeration
- versatile framing

### Best For

- dialogue
- documentaries
- street photography
- handheld sequences
- everyday storytelling

### Avoid When

- strong background compression is desired
- highly isolated portraits

### Cinematography Principle

A 35mm lens maintains a believable perspective while still showing enough of the surrounding environment to support storytelling. It is often considered one of the most versatile cinematic focal lengths.

### Example

Two friends walking through a busy street remain the focus while the city naturally surrounds them.

---

## 50mm

### Description

A standard lens producing a perspective close to natural human vision.

### Characteristics

- neutral perspective
- balanced proportions
- minimal distortion
- natural viewer perception

### Best For

- portraits
- conversations
- narrative filmmaking
- interviews
- general storytelling

### Avoid When

- dramatic environmental emphasis is required
- extreme telephoto compression is desired

### Cinematography Principle

A 50mm lens produces images that feel familiar and unobtrusive. It allows viewers to focus on characters rather than noticing the lens itself, making it an excellent default choice.

### Example

A quiet conversation across a dinner table feels intimate without exaggerating either the room or the faces.

---

## 85mm

### Description

A short telephoto lens that isolates subjects through perspective compression and shallow depth of field.

### Characteristics

- compressed perspective
- shallow depth of field
- strong subject isolation
- flattering facial proportions

### Best For

- emotional close-ups
- beauty shots
- fashion
- romantic scenes
- dramatic portraits

### Avoid When

- environmental storytelling is important
- confined interior spaces

### Cinematography Principle

An 85mm lens compresses spatial relationships and separates the subject from the background. This naturally draws the audience's attention toward facial expressions and emotional performance.

### Example

An emotional close-up allows the character's expression to dominate the frame while the background melts into soft blur.

---

# Lens Selection Rules

If emotional intimacy is the priority,
prefer **85mm**.

If environmental storytelling is important,
prefer **24–35mm**.

If realism and versatility are desired,
prefer **50mm**.

If no lens is specified,
default to **50mm**.
