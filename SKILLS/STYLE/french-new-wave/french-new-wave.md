# French New Wave

## Purpose

French New Wave (La Nouvelle Vague) is a cinematic movement centered on creative freedom, personal authorship, spontaneity, experimentation, and the rejection of rigid cinematic conventions.

Rather than pursuing invisible, technically perfect filmmaking, French New Wave embraces the presence of the filmmaker. Camera movement, editing, composition, performance, and narrative structure become expressive tools that reveal a personal perspective.

Use French New Wave when a project should feel youthful, curious, intimate, playful, intellectually engaged, and creatively unpredictable.

The final result should feel like a filmmaker discovering, questioning, and expressing the world rather than simply recording it.

---

# Historical Context

French New Wave emerged in France during the late 1950s and 1960s through filmmakers associated with *Cahiers du Cinéma*, including Jean-Luc Godard, François Truffaut, Agnès Varda, Claude Chabrol, Jacques Rivette, and Éric Rohmer.

Rejecting the polished conventions of the "Tradition of Quality," these filmmakers embraced lightweight cameras, location shooting, natural lighting, personal storytelling, improvisation, and experimental editing.

French New Wave challenged traditional cinematic grammar and transformed filmmaking into a more personal and expressive art form.

Its influence continues through independent cinema, auteur filmmaking, coming-of-age films, experimental narratives, and modern character-driven cinema.

---

# Core Philosophy

French New Wave values creative freedom over technical perfection.

Prioritize:

- personal expression over convention
- spontaneity over control
- experimentation over formula
- emotional truth over polished presentation
- curiosity over certainty
- cinematic playfulness over rigid structure
- individual perspective over objective observation

Imperfection should feel intentional and expressive rather than accidental.

The goal is not to create a flawed imitation of professional filmmaking.

The goal is to reveal a unique creative voice.

---

# Cinematic Law

French New Wave makes the filmmaking process visible.

The camera should not feel like a neutral recording device.

It should feel like a filmmaker with opinions, curiosity, humor, and emotion exploring the world.

The filmmaker's presence should be felt through:

- unexpected framing choices
- unusual camera movement
- unconventional editing
- spontaneous interactions
- personal visual decisions

Visual imperfections, creative interruptions, and broken conventions are not mistakes.

They are evidence of personal authorship.

The audience should sense the person behind the camera.

---

# Interpretation Priority

When translating French New Wave into a prompt, prioritize artistic decisions in the following order:

1. Authorial perspective
2. Camera personality
3. Editing language
4. Performance spontaneity
5. Composition
6. Narrative structure
7. Lighting approach
8. Production design
9. Visual texture

If a choice makes the film feel more personal and expressive, prefer it over technical perfection.

---

# Visual Language

French New Wave transforms everyday reality through personal cinematic expression.

Common characteristics include:

- real-world locations
- city streets
- cafés
- apartments
- bookstores
- public spaces
- youthful urban environments
- casual interiors
- minimalist production design
- natural environments
- spontaneous encounters
- unconventional framing
- playful visual balance
- direct connection between audience and character
- visible cinematic choices

Frames should feel discovered rather than manufactured.

Avoid overly designed compositions or artificial cinematic perfection.

The world should feel ordinary, but the filmmaker's perspective should make it meaningful.

---

# Camera

The camera should feel curious, energetic, and personally involved.

Favor:

- handheld cinematography
- lightweight camera movement
- spontaneous reframing
- unexpected pans
- long tracking shots
- fluid movement through real locations
- casual zooms
- sudden changes in perspective
- direct engagement with subjects
- unconventional framing
- expressive close-ups
- imperfect but intentional compositions

The camera should feel like a person exploring the world.

The camera may react emotionally rather than objectively.

It can hesitate, interrupt, observe unexpectedly, or choose unusual viewpoints because the filmmaker is expressing curiosity or a personal reaction.

Avoid:

- overly stabilized movement
- sterile perfection
- commercial camera choreography
- anonymous coverage-style filmmaking
- excessive visual polish
- invisible classical coverage

The camera should feel alive rather than controlled.

---

# Lens and Capture Characteristics

Favor a lightweight, accessible filmmaking feeling.

Use:

- natural perspective lenses
- realistic depth of field
- moderate optical imperfections
- handheld intimacy
- direct visual connection
- flexible camera positioning
- documentary-like immediacy

Avoid:

- overly luxurious cinema optics
- excessive anamorphic effects
- artificial blockbuster aesthetics
- highly polished commercial imagery

The image should feel immediate, personal, and expressive.

---

# Lighting

Lighting should preserve spontaneity and realism.

Favor:

- available light
- daylight
- window light
- practical interior lighting
- natural shadows
- realistic exposure
- soft environmental illumination
- authentic color variation
- imperfect lighting conditions

Avoid:

- dramatic studio lighting
- carefully sculpted Hollywood lighting
- artificial glamour lighting
- exaggerated visual mood
- overly controlled illumination

Lighting should support freedom rather than dominate the image.

---

# Composition

Composition should feel expressive rather than perfectly controlled.

Favor:

- unconventional framing
- asymmetrical balance
- casual camera placement
- characters entering and leaving the frame
- unexpected visual relationships
- negative space
- spontaneous arrangements
- everyday environments transformed through perspective
- unusual visual connections between characters and locations

The frame should communicate the filmmaker's curiosity.

Avoid:

- perfect symmetry
- overly polished visual design
- predictable cinematic composition

---

# Editing

Editing is one of the defining tools of French New Wave.

Editing should express freedom, thought, and personal rhythm rather than simply preserve continuity.

Favor:

- jump cuts
- discontinuity editing
- elliptical storytelling
- abrupt transitions
- playful pacing
- temporal experimentation
- unexpected scene structure
- breaking classical continuity
- moments of self-awareness
- associative editing

Editing should create rhythm, thought, and emotion.

Avoid using techniques mechanically.

A jump cut should express:

- a change in thought
- emotional disruption
- creative rhythm
- subjective perception

A stylistic technique should always feel like a filmmaker's decision.

Avoid:

- random cuts without purpose
- modern social-media editing
- music-video pacing
- excessive montage

Experimental editing should feel authored, not chaotic.

---

# Narrative Structure

French New Wave often rejects traditional dramatic structure.

Favor:

- episodic storytelling
- casual conversations
- wandering narratives
- incomplete resolutions
- character observation over plot mechanics
- philosophical discussions
- moments that exist for emotional or intellectual exploration
- everyday experiences becoming meaningful

Avoid:

- rigid three-act structure
- formulaic character arcs
- predictable dramatic progression
- excessive explanation

The story should feel discovered rather than engineered.

---

# Performance Style

Characters should feel alive and spontaneous.

Favor:

- natural dialogue
- conversational performances
- emotional immediacy
- playful interactions
- intellectual curiosity
- imperfect human behavior
- direct emotional expression
- moments of hesitation and uncertainty

Avoid:

- overly theatrical acting
- perfectly controlled performances
- artificial dramatic delivery
- exaggerated emotional performance

Characters should feel like people discovering themselves.

# Common Mistakes

Avoid reducing French New Wave to surface-level visual clichés.

French New Wave is not simply:

- handheld camera
- jump cuts
- black-and-white photography
- Paris locations
- vintage clothing
- retro aesthetics

Avoid:

- generic indie-film appearance
- artificial 1960s nostalgia
- polished commercial cinematography
- perfectly composed frames
- formulaic storytelling
- random experimentation
- spectacle without personal perspective
- excessive visual effects
- imitation without authorship
- style without meaning

Also avoid:

- invisible classical continuity
- perfectly motivated camera coverage
- emotionally manipulative filmmaking
- clean genre storytelling
- excessive narrative explanation

French New Wave should feel like cinema questioning itself.

The result should feel like a filmmaker expressing an individual voice, not a modern production imitating an old style.
