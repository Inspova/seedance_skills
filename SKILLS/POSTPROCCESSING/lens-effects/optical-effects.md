# Optical Effects

## Purpose

Optical effects simulate the physical behavior of real-world lenses, light, and transparent materials. Rather than acting as decorative visual filters, they recreate the subtle imperfections and interactions that naturally occur during image formation.

Unlike image finishing, which polishes the final image, optical effects originate from photographic optics themselves, increasing realism, cinematic authenticity, or artistic expression.

Use optical effects to strengthen immersion, depth, and photographic credibility while remaining consistent with the scene's lighting, camera, and lens characteristics.

---

# Anamorphic Flares

## Sample Prompt

Add subtle anamorphic lens flares with horizontal light streaks created by bright light sources interacting with anamorphic optics. Preserve realistic light intensity while introducing elegant cinematic streaks that enhance production value without overwhelming the image.

---

## Essence

Cinematic lens behavior produced by anamorphic optics.

---

## Characteristics

- horizontal light streaks
- elongated lens flares
- subtle blue or amber highlights
- cinematic lens artifacts
- controlled flare intensity
- premium cinematic appearance
- optical realism

---

## Best For

- science fiction
- action films
- cyberpunk
- futuristic cities
- night photography
- automotive cinematography
- strong practical lighting

---

## Avoid When

- documentary realism
- historical dramas
- minimalist photography
- scenes without strong light sources

---

# Chromatic Aberration

## Sample Prompt

Apply subtle chromatic aberration by introducing delicate color fringing along high-contrast edges. Simulate authentic lens imperfections while preserving overall image sharpness and maintaining a natural photographic appearance.

---

## Essence

Natural optical imperfections that add lens character.

---

## Characteristics

- red, green, or blue edge separation
- authentic lens imperfections
- vintage optical character
- slight edge distortion
- realistic photographic artifacts

# Optical Effects Guidelines

- Apply optical effects only when they naturally arise from the scene or intentionally support the visual style.
- Preserve physical plausibility whenever possible.
- Match optical behavior to the selected camera system and lens characteristics.
- Use lens imperfections sparingly to avoid distracting from the subject.
- Maintain consistency across the entire sequence.
- Reflections and refractions should accurately respond to lighting, viewing angle, and surrounding environments.
- Favor subtle realism over exaggerated visual effects.

---

# Final Principle

Optical effects should behave as natural consequences of photography rather than decorative overlays.

Their purpose is to strengthen realism, depth, and cinematic authenticity through believable interactions between light, lenses, and transparent materials.

The most convincing optical effects are those that viewers instinctively accept as having been captured by the camera itself.
