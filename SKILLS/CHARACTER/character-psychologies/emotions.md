# Emotions

## Purpose

Choose emotions based on the character's current situation.

Emotions are temporary internal states that influence thoughts, decisions, and behavior.

Prefer nuanced emotions over simplistic labels whenever appropriate.

---

# Joy

## Sample Prompt

Portray a character experiencing joy through natural expressions and relaxed body language. Show genuine happiness through subtle smiles, bright eyes, open posture, and emotionally warm interactions rather than exaggerated reactions.

## Examples

- happiness
- delight
- excitement
- relief
- gratitude
- amusement
- contentment
- pride
- serenity
- optimism

---

# Sadness

## Sample Prompt

Portray sadness through restrained emotional expression, slower movement, lowered energy, and reflective behavior. Avoid exaggerated crying unless the narrative requires it; focus on internal emotional weight and vulnerability.

## Examples

- grief
- loneliness
- regret
- melancholy
- despair
- heartbreak
- hopelessness

# Emotional Progression

## Sample Prompt

Create a natural emotional transition where a character's feelings evolve over time based on events and personal experience. Show gradual changes through behavior, expression, and decisions rather than sudden unexplained emotional shifts.

## Examples

Fear → Courage

Anger → Regret

Grief → Acceptance

Loneliness → Connection

Hope → Disappointment

Suspicion → Trust

Joy → Melancholy

---

# Selection Rules

## Sample Prompt

Select emotions according to the character's situation, personality, and narrative context. Prefer subtle and believable emotional states, allowing mixed emotions and internal contradictions when appropriate.

---

If the situation is uncertain:

- prefer restrained emotions over extremes

If multiple emotions coexist:

- allow them to overlap naturally

Mixed emotions often feel more believable than a single dominant emotion.

---

# Final Principle

Emotion should be expressed through the entire character system:

1. Emotion
   ↓
2. Facial expression
   ↓
3. Body language
   ↓
4. Movement
   ↓
5. Dialogue / action
   ↓
6. Relationship behavior

Avoid treating emotions as isolated facial expressions.

A convincing emotional state should influence how a character looks, moves, communicates, and interacts with the world.
