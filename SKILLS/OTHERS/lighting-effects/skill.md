# Lighting

## Purpose

This module governs all lighting-related decisions for Seedance.

Its purpose is to produce lighting that is intentional, believable, and emotionally expressive. Every lighting decision should support the story, enhance the subject, and reinforce the mood without distracting from the scene.

This module coordinates four aspects of lighting:

- **Quality** — the visual character and contrast of the light
- **Direction** — where the light originates relative to the subject
- **Source** — what produces the light within the scene
- **Color** — the color temperature and emotional tone of the light

---

# Intent

Think like a professional cinematographer and gaffer.

Lighting should appear motivated whenever possible, as though it naturally exists within the world of the scene. Stylized lighting is acceptable when it intentionally supports the narrative or artistic direction.

Every lighting choice should help the viewer understand where they should look and how they should feel.

---

# Lighting Contract

Lighting should obey the following principles:

- remain physically believable unless intentionally stylized
- maintain consistent direction throughout the shot
- preserve logical relationships between light and shadow
- use motivated light sources whenever practical
- avoid unexplained changes in illumination
- support subject visibility without overwhelming the composition

Unless explicitly requested, lighting should feel natural and unobtrusive.

---

# Decision Process

When designing lighting, make decisions in the following order.

## 1. What emotional tone should the scene convey?

Examples:

- warmth
- intimacy
- hope
- mystery
- suspense
- fear
- loneliness
- grandeur

Select the appropriate light quality from **quality.md**.

---

## 2. Where should the light come from?

Determine the direction that best supports the subject and reinforces the intended mood.

Consult **direction.md**.

---

## 3. What motivates the light?

Choose one or more believable light sources within the environment.

Examples:

- sunlight
- window light
- candlelight
- neon
- street lamps
- practical lighting

Consult **sources.md**.

---

## 4. What color should the light be?

Select the appropriate color temperature or lighting color that supports the scene.

Consult **color.md**.

---

# Lighting Hierarchy

When lighting decisions conflict, prioritize:

1. Subject visibility
2. Narrative intent
3. Lighting continuity
4. Physical realism
5. Visual style

Lower priorities should never compromise higher priorities.

---

# Continuity

Within a single shot:

- maintain consistent light direction
- preserve light intensity
- maintain consistent color temperature
- preserve believable shadows
- avoid sudden lighting changes unless narratively motivated

Lighting transitions should feel smooth and physically plausible.

---

# Default Behaviour

If no lighting instructions are provided:

- use natural, motivated lighting
- use soft light
- use a neutral color temperature
- illuminate the primary subject clearly
- maintain balanced contrast
- avoid overly dramatic or stylized lighting

---

# Output

When describing lighting, specify only the lighting properties that materially affect the scene.

Where applicable, include:

- light quality
- light direction
- light source
- light color
- overall mood

Avoid redundant, contradictory, or physically implausible lighting descriptions.

---

# Lighting Philosophy

Lighting should feel like part of the world rather than an effect applied to it.

Quality, direction, source, and color should work together to create a unified visual language.

Prefer motivated, coherent lighting over excessive stylistic complexity. Every lighting decision should support the same emotional objective and reinforce the story.
