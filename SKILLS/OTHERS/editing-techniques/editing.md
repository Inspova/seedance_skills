# Editing

## Purpose

Editing shapes how the audience experiences time, rhythm, and narrative progression.

Every edit should serve a clear storytelling purpose, whether maintaining continuity, controlling pacing, emphasizing emotion, or connecting ideas.

Choose editing techniques based on narrative intent rather than visual novelty.

---

# Transitions

## Dissolve

## Sample Prompt

A dissolve is a gradual transition where one image fades out as another fades in, with both briefly visible simultaneously. This technique suggests passage of time, changes in location, or thematic connections between scenes. Short dissolves create subtle transitions, while longer dissolves create a dreamlike and emotional feeling.

## Purpose

- suggest passage of time
- connect related moments
- create a dreamlike transition

## Best For

- memories
- romance
- scene transitions
- reflective moments

---

## Fade In / Fade Out

## Sample Prompt

A fade is a gradual transition between an image and a solid color, typically black. Fade-ins usually introduce scenes or films, while fade-outs close them, suggesting beginnings, endings, or significant time passage. Fade-to-white transitions can create dreamlike or symbolic effects.

## Purpose

- signify beginnings or endings
- indicate major time jumps
- provide emotional closure

## Best For

- opening scenes
- ending scenes
- chapter breaks

---

## Smash Cut

## Sample Prompt

A smash cut is an abrupt transition between two contrasting scenes or moments. It creates a sudden shift that can produce surprise, comedy, dramatic irony, or emotional impact by emphasizing the contrast between the two shots.

## Purpose

- surprise the audience
- create dramatic irony
- emphasize sudden change

## Best For

- comedy
- horror
- action
- dramatic reveals

---

# Continuity

## Match Cut

## Sample Prompt

A match cut transitions between shots by matching visual elements such as shapes, colors, movement, or composition. This technique creates visual continuity while jumping across time or space, allowing unrelated scenes to become connected through visual or thematic relationships.

## Purpose

- create elegant visual continuity
- connect ideas across time or space

## Best For

- symbolic storytelling
- creative transitions
- visual metaphors

# Audio-Visual Continuity

## J-Cut

## Sample Prompt

A J-cut introduces audio from the incoming scene before the visual transition occurs. The audience hears the next scene before seeing it, creating anticipation, smoother transitions, and stronger emotional continuity. This technique is especially effective for dialogue transitions and preparing the audience for a new location or event.

## Purpose

- prepare the audience for the next scene
- create anticipation
- smooth scene transitions

## Best For

- dialogue
- scene changes
- documentaries

---

## L-Cut

## Sample Prompt

An L-cut allows audio from the previous scene to continue after the visual transition. Sound from the previous shot overlaps with the next image, creating emotional continuity, smoother pacing, and connections between different moments.

## Purpose

- maintain emotional continuity
- soften transitions
- connect conversations

## Best For

- dialogue
- emotional scenes
- reflective moments

---

# Rhythm

## Jump Cut

## Sample Prompt

A jump cut is an abrupt transition that breaks continuous action and creates intentional discontinuity within a scene. Unlike seamless continuity editing, jump cuts draw attention to the edit itself. They can compress time, increase energy, express psychological states, or create an artistic rhythm.

## Purpose

- compress time
- create energy
- express psychological instability

## Best For

- artistic films
- fast pacing
- internal monologue
- comedy

---

## Long Take

## Sample Prompt

A long take is an extended shot lasting significantly longer than typical editing rhythm. It presents events in continuous real time, immersing the audience, preserving performance and movement, and creating a distinctive cinematic rhythm.

## Purpose

- immerse the audience
- build tension
- preserve real-time continuity

## Best For

- dramatic performances
- choreography
- suspense
- realism

---

## Montage

## Sample Prompt

A montage combines multiple short shots into a sequence that compresses time, conveys progression, or creates meaning through the relationship between images. It can show character development, journeys, transformation, or changes over time through visual accumulation.

## Purpose

- summarize events
- show development
- communicate transformation

## Best For

- training sequences
- travel
- romance
- character growth

# Narrative Structure

## Cross-Cutting

## Sample Prompt

Cross-cutting, also known as parallel editing, alternates between two or more simultaneous storylines happening in different locations. This technique creates tension by showing events occurring at the same time, builds suspense, and can establish thematic connections between separate events.

## Purpose

- build suspense
- compare parallel events
- create dramatic tension

## Best For

- rescue sequences
- chase scenes
- parallel narratives

---

## Split Screen

## Sample Prompt

Split screen divides the frame to display multiple images simultaneously, often showing related events, conversations, or contrasting actions. The technique openly embraces its visual construction and creates dynamic compositions by connecting multiple elements within the same frame.

## Purpose

- compare actions
- show simultaneous events
- emphasize relationships

## Best For

- phone conversations
- parallel action
- artistic storytelling

---

## Freeze Frame

## Sample Prompt

A freeze frame holds on a single frame, stopping motion while time appears frozen. This technique emphasizes important moments, creates dramatic pauses, introduces characters, or punctuates endings and significant events.

## Purpose

- emphasize important moments
- create dramatic pauses
- punctuate endings

## Best For

- introductions
- climaxes
- endings

---

# Editing Guidelines

- Every cut should have a clear narrative purpose.
- Prefer invisible continuity editing unless stylistic editing is intentionally desired.
- Match editing rhythm to the emotional pacing of the scene.
- Preserve spatial and temporal clarity unless intentional disorientation supports the story.
- Use transitions sparingly; excessive editing techniques reduce their impact.
- Choose the simplest edit that best serves the narrative.
- Editing style should support story, emotion, and audience experience rather than visual novelty.
