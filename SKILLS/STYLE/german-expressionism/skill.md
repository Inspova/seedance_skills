# German Expressionism

## Purpose

This skill governs the application of German Expressionism as a complete cinematic movement for Seedance.

German Expressionism is not simply distorted architecture, horror imagery, exaggerated shadows, or gothic aesthetics. It is a cinematic philosophy that transforms internal psychological states into external visual reality.

When activated, the world itself should become a reflection of emotion, fear, anxiety, madness, obsession, or existential uncertainty. Architecture, lighting, composition, performance, and atmosphere should feel psychologically motivated rather than physically realistic.

The final image should feel like the viewer has entered a character's disturbed inner world.

# Application Philosophy

Treat German Expressionism as a complete psychological visual system.

Its influence should extend across:

- production design
- architecture
- composition
- lighting
- staging
- camera perspective
- performance
- atmosphere
- visual symbolism

Every creative decision should externalize the emotional condition of the characters.

The environment should not simply contain the story. It should psychologically represent the story.

Prefer symbolic distortion over realistic representation when emotional truth requires it.

---

# Interpretation Priority

When translating German Expressionism into a prompt, prioritize artistic decisions in the following order:

1. Symbolic environment design
2. Composition and perspective distortion
3. Lighting and shadow design
4. Psychological atmosphere
5. Staging and performance
6. Camera behavior
7. Editing rhythm
8. Realistic physical accuracy

If realism conflicts with emotional expression, choose psychological symbolism.

# Visual Language

German Expressionism transforms ordinary reality into a psychological landscape.

Common characteristics include:

- impossible architecture
- sharp geometric forms
- warped perspectives
- unnatural spaces
- exaggerated scale
- claustrophobic environments
- painted or theatrical backgrounds
- extreme contrast
- symbolic objects
- threatening shadows
- visual imbalance
- unsettling compositions
- distorted spatial relationships

The world should feel emotionally charged rather than physically neutral.

---

# Production Design

Production design is one of the defining elements of German Expressionism.

Favor:

- crooked buildings
- angular architecture
- impossible staircases
- distorted streets
- exaggerated interiors
- oppressive rooms
- unnatural landscapes
- symbolic objects
- theatrical environments
- abstract shapes
- painted textures
- surreal spatial relationships

Environments should communicate psychological states.

A room can feel paranoid.
A hallway can feel endless.
A building can feel hostile.

---

# Composition

Composition should create psychological discomfort.

Favor:

- extreme perspective
- diagonal lines
- tilted horizons
- unusual framing
- visual imbalance
- overwhelming scale
- characters trapped by surroundings
- exaggerated depth
- strong geometric patterns
- oppressive negative space

Avoid neutral or comfortable framing.

The image should feel emotionally unstable.

---

# Lighting

Lighting should express inner emotion rather than simply illuminate reality.

Favor:

- dramatic shadows
- unnatural light direction
- sharp contrast
- theatrical illumination
- symbolic darkness
- exaggerated silhouettes
- isolated pools of light
- extreme highlights
- threatening shadow patterns

Light should feel expressive and psychologically motivated.

---

# Camera

Camera behavior should reinforce the distorted world.

Favor:

- unusual angles
- low-angle perspectives
- extreme close-ups
- unsettling framing
- slow movement through impossible spaces
- deliberate visual tension
- subjective camera perspectives

Avoid:

- neutral documentary observation
- ordinary cinematic coverage
- purely realistic camera language

The camera should experience the world psychologically.

---

# Performance Style

Performances should reflect heightened emotional states.

Favor:

- expressive body language
- exaggerated gestures
- theatrical movement
- psychological intensity
- symbolic poses
- unnatural stillness

Avoid:

- completely naturalistic acting
- casual realism
- restrained documentary performance

The human body should become part of the visual expression.

---

# Atmosphere

The atmosphere should feel psychologically oppressive.

Common elements include:

- dread
- paranoia
- isolation
- nightmare logic
- anxiety
- obsession
- uncertainty
- supernatural ambiguity
- emotional instability

The audience should feel trapped inside the character's perception.

# Common Mistakes

Avoid reducing German Expressionism to visual decoration.

German Expressionism is not simply:

- dark castles
- horror imagery
- black shadows
- gothic costumes
- creepy environments

Avoid:

- realistic production design
- conventional architecture
- naturalistic lighting
- ordinary camera perspective
- generic horror aesthetics
- excessive visual effects
- random distortion without meaning

Every distortion should reveal something psychological.

German Expressionism is the transformation of emotion into visual reality.

Its success is measured not by how strange the image looks, but by whether every creative decision—especially environment, composition, lighting, and perspective—makes the audience experience the psychological state of the character.
