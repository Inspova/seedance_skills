# Film Noir

## Purpose

This skill governs the application of Film Noir as a complete cinematic movement for Seedance.

Film Noir is not a visual filter, color preset, or collection of recognizable props. It is a complete cinematic system in which tonal rendering, lighting, composition, camera behavior, production design, atmosphere, and pacing create a world defined by secrecy, psychological tension, moral uncertainty, and emotional isolation.

When activated, this skill should transform the entire visual language of the scene into unmistakable Film Noir rather than simply making the image darker.

# Cinematic Law

Film Noir transforms reality into a world where truth is hidden and only partially revealed.

Darkness conceals information.
Light exposes fragments.
Every frame should suggest that something important remains unseen.

The visual language should create uncertainty, suspicion, isolation, and psychological tension through selective visibility.

The audience should feel that they are observing a world of secrets.

---

# When to Use

Activate this skill whenever the user explicitly requests Film Noir or when the creative objective naturally aligns with classic or neo-noir storytelling.

Typical indicators include:

- detective stories
- crime dramas
- murder mysteries
- psychological thrillers
- espionage
- corruption
- revenge
- betrayal
- moral ambiguity
- fatalism
- urban loneliness
- neo-noir
- suspense
- classic crime cinema atmosphere

Do not activate this skill solely because the user requests:

- black-and-white imagery
- dramatic shadows
- dark scenes
- rainy streets

Darkness alone does not constitute Film Noir.

---

# Application Philosophy

Treat Film Noir as a complete visual language.

Its influence should consistently shape:

- tonal rendering
- contrast
- lighting
- atmosphere
- composition
- camera behavior
- production design
- character presentation
- editing rhythm
- visual texture

Every artistic decision should reinforce:

- secrecy
- psychological tension
- moral uncertainty
- loneliness
- fatalistic atmosphere

Avoid creating a modern dark thriller with isolated noir elements.

The entire image should feel as if it belongs to the Film Noir tradition.

---

# Interpretation Priority

When translating Film Noir into a prompt, prioritize artistic decisions in the following order:

1. Tonal rendering and photographic character
2. Lighting and shadow structure
3. Atmospheric environment
4. Composition and framing
5. Camera behavior
6. Production design
7. Character presentation
8. Editing rhythm

If realism, brightness, or modern cinematic polish conflicts with noir atmosphere, preserve the noir visual identity.

# Vintage Cinematic Identity

Classic Film Noir should feel photographed through a historical cinematic process rather than captured by a modern digital camera.

Favor:

- black-and-white silver gelatin photography
- visible film grain
- high contrast exposure
- crushed blacks
- slightly soft vintage lenses
- optical imperfections
- subtle film aging
- atmospheric haze
- period photographic texture

Avoid:

- clean digital sharpness
- modern HDR contrast
- glossy commercial cinematography
- contemporary music-video aesthetics

The image should feel like a surviving frame from mid-century crime cinema.

---

# Lighting

Lighting is the defining visual force of Film Noir.

Favor:

- extreme chiaroscuro
- hard directional light
- deep shadows
- practical lamps
- streetlights
- window light
- silhouettes
- isolated pools of illumination
- dramatic backlighting
- venetian blind shadows
- reflections on wet surfaces

Darkness should actively hide information.

Avoid evenly illuminated scenes.

---

# Style Boundaries

Avoid reducing Film Noir to visual clichés.

Film Noir is not defined only by:

- black-and-white imagery
- cigarettes
- trench coats
- detectives
- rainy streets
- venetian blinds

These elements should only appear when they reinforce the larger cinematic philosophy.

Avoid:

- generic dark thriller aesthetics
- clean modern environments
- bright commercial lighting
- colorful fantasy aesthetics
- action-film pacing
- excessive visual effects

The goal is psychological tension, not decoration.

---

# Integration

Film Noir should establish the dominant visual language whenever active.

When combined with other filmmaking skills, Film Noir should control:

- tonal rendering
- contrast
- lighting
- atmosphere
- composition

Compatible styles may influence narrative or subject matter, but they should not weaken the noir photographic identity.

---

# Default Behavior

If Film Noir is neither explicitly requested nor naturally appropriate for the narrative, do not activate this skill.

Maintain visual neutrality and allow other cinematic movement skills to determine the overall style.

---

# Final Principle

Film Noir is the art of hiding truth inside darkness.

Its success is measured not by noir objects or crime imagery, but by whether every cinematic decision creates a world of uncertainty, secrecy, isolation, and psychological tension through shadow, contrast, atmosphere, and photographic character.
