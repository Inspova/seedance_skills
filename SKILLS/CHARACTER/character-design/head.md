# Head

## Purpose

Define facial appearance, facial features, micro-expressions, and head-level visual details that create believable characters.

Head design affects:

- identity
- personality
- age perception
- emotional readability
- realism
- cinematic intimacy

A convincing face should not look digitally perfect.

Prioritize:

- natural skin variation
- realistic anatomy
- subtle asymmetry
- micro-details
- interaction with lighting
- emotional expression

Avoid:

- plastic skin
- porcelain appearance
- artificial symmetry
- excessive beauty retouching

---

# Eyes

## Realistic Eyes

Characteristics:

- natural iris patterns
- visible iris texture
- realistic pupil depth
- subtle sclera blood vessels
- natural moisture
- small catchlights
- irregular eyelashes

Best for:

- emotional close-ups
- character introductions
- intimate scenes
- psychological moments

Avoid:

- glass-like eyes
- perfectly white sclera
- artificial symmetry

Example:

> Extreme close-up portrait of a character with realistic iris patterns, subtle eye moisture, tiny blood vessels in the sclera, natural eyelashes, and soft cinematic catchlights reflected in the pupils.

---

## Tearful Eyes

Characteristics:

- tears covering the eye surface
- enlarged soft highlights
- slightly red lower eyelids
- eyelashes sticking together naturally
- reflective moisture layer

Creates:

- sadness
- vulnerability
- emotional release
- suppressed emotion

Example:

> A close-up of a character holding back tears, watery eyes reflecting soft light, slightly red lower eyelids, damp eyelashes naturally clumped together, showing restrained sadness.

---

## Trembling Eyelids

Characteristics:

- subtle eyelid movement
- thin eyelid folds
- small skin tension changes
- eyelashes lightly touching skin

Creates:

- hesitation
- fear
- exhaustion
- emotional restraint

Example:

> A nervous character closes their eyes briefly, trembling eyelids with visible fine folds, eyelashes moving slightly as they struggle to control their emotions.

---

## Eye Direction

Eye direction communicates psychology.

### Direct Eye Contact

Creates:

- confidence
- intimacy
- confrontation
- connection

Example:

> The character looks directly into the camera with calm confidence, maintaining intense eye contact that creates emotional connection.

### Looking Away

Creates:

- uncertainty
- avoidance
- reflection
- emotional distance

Example:

> A character looks away toward the window, avoiding eye contact while appearing lost in thought.

### Looking Down

Creates:

- sadness
- submission
- contemplation
- vulnerability

Example:

> The character lowers their gaze, eyes partially hidden by eyelashes, expressing quiet sadness and emotional vulnerability.

# Head Close-Up Rules

For cinematic close-ups prioritize:

1. eyes
2. expression
3. skin texture
4. lighting interaction
5. small imperfections

Avoid describing only beauty.

A strong character face should communicate:

- who they are
- what they feel
- what they have experienced

Example:

> Cinematic close-up portrait of a character showing emotional eyes, realistic skin texture, subtle facial tension, natural imperfections, and lighting that reveals personality.

---

# Output Guidance

When describing a character's head, specify:

- facial structure
- eyes
- eyebrows
- lips
- skin condition
- makeup
- hair interaction
- distinctive marks
- emotional state

A realistic face is created through controlled imperfections, not perfection.
