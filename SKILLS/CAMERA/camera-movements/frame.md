# Framing

## Purpose

Choose framing based on the emotional and narrative purpose of the scene.

Framing determines how much information the viewer receives, how close they feel to the subject, and what visual elements compete for attention.

A closer frame increases emotional intimacy and psychological focus.
A wider frame emphasizes environment, relationships, and spatial context.

---

# Extreme Wide Shot (EWS)

## Description

An extreme wide shot shows the subject as a small element within a large environment.

## Characteristics

- very large field of view
- subject occupies a small portion of the frame
- strong emphasis on location and scale
- creates distance between viewer and subject

## Best For

- establishing locations
- showing isolation
- emphasizing scale
- landscapes
- epic moments

## Avoid When

- emotional intimacy is the priority
- facial expressions are important

## Cinematography Principle

An extreme wide shot makes the environment the dominant subject. It can make characters appear vulnerable, insignificant, lonely, or overwhelmed by their surroundings.

## Example

A lone traveler walking through a vast desert emphasizes isolation and the overwhelming scale of nature.

---

# Wide Shot (WS)

## Description

A wide shot shows the full subject while preserving significant environmental context.

## Characteristics

- full body visible
- clear relationship between subject and location
- balanced subject and environment
- strong sense of physical space

## Best For

- introductions
- movement through environments
- action choreography
- group scenes
- cinematic compositions

## Avoid When

- subtle facial emotion is the focus

## Cinematography Principle

A wide shot allows viewers to understand both the character and their surroundings. It establishes physical relationships and helps maintain spatial clarity.

## Example

A character entering an unfamiliar room is shown together with the architecture, furniture, and atmosphere of the location.

---

# Medium Wide Shot (MWS)

## Description

A medium wide shot frames the subject approximately from the knees upward.

## Characteristics

- preserves body language
- maintains environmental context
- allows natural movement
- balanced emotional and spatial information

## Best For

- dialogue scenes
- walking shots
- character interactions
- cinematic coverage

## Avoid When

- extreme emotional detail is required

## Cinematography Principle

A medium wide shot balances personality and environment. It allows viewers to read gestures, posture, and relationships while still maintaining connection with the character.

## Example

Two characters speaking in a hallway are framed from the knees upward, allowing both facial expressions and body language to communicate.

---

# Medium Shot (MS)

## Description

A medium shot typically frames the subject from the waist or chest upward.

## Characteristics

- natural conversational distance
- balanced face and body visibility
- comfortable viewer relationship
- commonly used narrative framing

## Best For

- conversations
- interviews
- everyday scenes
- character-driven moments

## Avoid When

- strong environmental storytelling is needed
- extreme emotional intensity is required

## Cinematography Principle

A medium shot creates a familiar human perspective. It allows audiences to connect with characters while still observing gestures and interactions.

## Example

A conversation between two characters uses medium shots to maintain emotional connection without feeling invasive.

---

# Medium Close-Up (MCU)

## Description

A medium close-up frames the subject from the chest or shoulders upward.

## Characteristics

- emphasizes facial expression
- maintains some body language
- increases emotional connection
- reduces environmental distraction

## Best For

- dialogue
- emotional reactions
- interviews
- character moments

## Avoid When

- the environment is narratively important

## Cinematography Principle

A medium close-up increases psychological intimacy while maintaining enough physical context to understand the character's presence.

## Example

A character receiving unexpected news is shown in a medium close-up, allowing the audience to observe both expression and subtle posture changes.

---

# Close-Up (CU)

## Description

A close-up tightly frames the subject's face or an important object.

## Characteristics

- strong emotional focus
- removes environmental distraction
- highlights subtle details
- increases psychological intensity

## Best For

- emotional reactions
- important dialogue
- character decisions
- dramatic moments

## Avoid When

- spatial relationships need to be understood

## Cinematography Principle

A close-up forces the viewer to focus on emotional details. Small changes in expression become significant because the camera removes external distractions.

## Example

A close-up reveals a character trying to hide fear through a controlled expression.

---

# Extreme Close-Up (ECU)

## Description

An extreme close-up isolates a very small detail such as eyes, lips, hands, or an object.

## Characteristics

- extreme detail
- heightened emotional intensity
- abstract visual quality
- removes broader context

## Best For

- symbolic details
- emotional peaks
- suspense
- visual storytelling

## Avoid When

- natural conversation or spatial clarity is required

## Cinematography Principle

An extreme close-up transforms a small detail into a major storytelling element. It directs the audience's attention toward something psychologically or narratively important.

## Example

A close-up of trembling fingers reveals nervousness before the character speaks.

---

# Two Shot

## Description

A two shot frames two characters together within the same composition.

## Characteristics

- emphasizes relationships
- preserves spatial connection
- shows reactions simultaneously
- avoids unnecessary cutting

## Best For

- conversations
- romantic scenes
- conflicts
- character dynamics

## Avoid When

- one character's internal emotion should dominate

## Cinematography Principle

A two shot allows the audience to observe the relationship between characters rather than only individual reactions.

## Example

Two characters sitting silently together are framed in the same shot, emphasizing emotional distance or connection.

---

# Over-The-Shoulder Shot (OTS)

## Description

An over-the-shoulder shot frames one character from behind another character's shoulder.

## Characteristics

- creates conversational perspective
- maintains spatial relationship
- increases viewer involvement
- common in dialogue scenes

## Best For

- conversations
- confrontations
- emotional exchanges

## Avoid When

- complete isolation of one subject is needed

## Cinematography Principle

An over-the-shoulder shot places the audience within the interaction, making them feel like a participant observing the conversation.

## Example

A character revealing a secret is framed over the listener's shoulder, increasing emotional involvement.

---

# POV (Point of View)

## Description

A POV shot shows the scene from a character's visual perspective.

## Characteristics

- subjective camera
- immersive experience
- connects viewer with character perception
- reveals information selectively

## Best For

- suspense
- discovery
- emotional identification
- first-person experiences

## Avoid When

- objective observation is preferred

## Cinematography Principle

A POV shot turns the audience into the character, controlling what information is seen and creating direct psychological connection.

## Example

A character entering a dark room is shown through their eyes, making the viewer discover the space with them.

---

# Framing Selection Rules

If emotional intimacy is the priority,
prefer:

- Close-Up
- Medium Close-Up
- Extreme Close-Up

If character and environment both matter,
prefer:

- Medium Shot
- Medium Wide Shot

If scale and atmosphere are important,
prefer:

- Wide Shot
- Extreme Wide Shot

If the relationship between characters matters,
prefer:

- Two Shot
- Over-The-Shoulder Shot

If no framing is specified,
default to:

**Medium Shot**

unless the emotional context requires a closer or wider perspective.
