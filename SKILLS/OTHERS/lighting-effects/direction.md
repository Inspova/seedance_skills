# Light Direction

## Purpose

Light direction determines how subjects are shaped and perceived.

Different lighting angles communicate different emotional and visual qualities.

---

## Front Lighting

## Sample Prompt

Use front lighting with the main light source positioned toward the subject to create even illumination with minimal shadows. This approach produces a clean, flattering appearance, reduces facial texture, and emphasizes clarity over dramatic depth.

## Characteristics

- evenly illuminates the subject
- minimizes shadows
- reduces facial texture

## Best For

- interviews
- beauty
- commercial work

---

## Side Lighting

## Sample Prompt

Use side lighting with the main light positioned from one side of the subject to create strong contrast between illuminated and shadow areas. This emphasizes facial structure, texture, depth, and emotional intensity.

## Characteristics

- strong light-shadow contrast
- emphasizes depth and texture

## Best For

- dramatic portraits
- mystery
- cinematic dialogue

---

## Back Lighting

## Sample Prompt

Use back lighting with the primary light source placed behind the subject to create separation from the background. Strong rim highlights and glowing outlines should emphasize silhouettes, scale, and dramatic reveals.

## Characteristics

- light originates behind the subject
- creates separation
- highlights outlines

## Best For

- silhouettes
- cinematic reveals
- hero shots

---

## Top Lighting

## Sample Prompt

Use top lighting with illumination coming from above the subject to create a natural overhead lighting effect. This can produce realistic daylight conditions or dramatic shadows depending on intensity and contrast.

## Characteristics

- light falls from above
- natural overhead appearance

## Best For

- daylight
- interrogation scenes
- dramatic interiors

---

## Bottom Lighting

## Sample Prompt

Use bottom lighting with the light source positioned below the subject to create unnatural upward shadows and a distorted appearance. This lighting direction should emphasize fear, unease, and supernatural atmosphere.

## Characteristics

- light shines upward
- unnatural appearance

## Best For

- horror
- supernatural scenes

## Three-Quarter Lighting

## Sample Prompt

Use three-quarter lighting with the main light positioned approximately 45 degrees from the subject. This creates balanced illumination with natural depth, gentle shadow shaping, and a cinematic portrait appearance.

## Characteristics

- light positioned approximately 45° from the subject
- balanced highlight and shadow
- natural facial depth

## Best For

- portraits
- interviews
- cinematic dialogue

---

## Rembrandt Lighting

## Sample Prompt

Use Rembrandt lighting with a directional key light creating a small triangular highlight beneath one eye on the shadowed side of the face. This classic portrait technique creates depth, drama, and a timeless cinematic appearance.

## Characteristics

- small illuminated triangle beneath one eye
- strong light-shadow relationship
- classic portrait style

## Best For

- dramatic portraits
- historical scenes

---

## Butterfly Lighting

## Sample Prompt

Use butterfly lighting with a centered light source placed slightly above the subject to create a symmetrical facial illumination and a butterfly-shaped shadow beneath the nose. This produces a polished, elegant, and glamorous portrait style.

## Characteristics

- centered overhead light
- butterfly-shaped shadow beneath the nose
- symmetrical facial lighting

## Best For

- fashion
- beauty
- glamour

---

## Loop Lighting

## Sample Prompt

Use loop lighting with the key light positioned slightly above and to the side of the subject, creating a small shadow beside the nose. This provides balanced facial modeling while maintaining a natural and approachable appearance.

## Characteristics

- small shadow beside the nose
- balanced depth
- natural facial shaping

## Best For

- portraits
- interviews

---

# Direction Guidelines

- Front lighting minimizes drama and creates clarity.
- Side lighting increases depth, texture, and emotional contrast.
- Back lighting separates subjects and creates cinematic silhouettes.
- Top lighting creates natural overhead illumination or dramatic shadow patterns.
- Bottom lighting should be used carefully because it creates unnatural visual perception.
- Choose lighting direction based on emotional intention, subject shape, and scene atmosphere.
- Combine lighting direction with light quality and color to create a complete cinematic lighting system.
